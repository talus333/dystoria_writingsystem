# The Long Tally — working notes

What I want this book to do: make a reader feel the weight of an obligation they never agreed to, and then make them feel what it costs to put it down. I want the debt system to be legible enough that the reader can do the arithmetic before Mirel does. And I want the marsh to feel like a place with weather, not a backdrop.

I'd like it to sit somewhere between a slow crime novel and a secondary-world fantasy — the magic, such as it is, should stay at the edge of the frame.

---

## People

**Mirel Sarn** — tally-warden, eleven years in the office. Careful. Guarded. Middle-aged. Her whole competence is a kind of hiding place. Wants the ledger to be true; the book is about what she does when she finds out it isn't.

**Corvin Ash** — smuggler, sort of. Married Mirel's sister Hessa, buried her, stayed. Charming and unreliable in the specific way of someone who has never been made to pay for anything. Scar across the right hand from a winch line. Younger than Mirel by four years.

**Dorne Vashet** — Prefect of Saltmarch, third of her house to hold the office. Not a villain. Believes the drowning-debt is the only thing holding the city together and is probably, in the narrow sense, correct.

**Old Halbrid** — ferryman at the Gullet crossing. Elderly, patient, sees everything, says a third of it. The book's only reliable witness.

**Vessel Doret** — senior officiant of the Communion of the Drowned. Ancient. Immovable. Speaks in refusals.

**Saint Ilva** — dead four hundred years, walked into the marsh in a famine year and did not come back. Not a character; a shape people argue about. Ilva's Landing is named for her.

**Hessa Sarn** — Mirel's sister, Corvin's wife, died in the year of the weir. Appears only in memory. Do not give her scenes.

**Wren** — the Vashet house-runner, a child of maybe twelve. Barely in the first draft. Might cut.

## Places

**Saltmarch** — the city. Built on a drowned field at the mouth of the Gullet; half of it is on piles. Cold, tidal, brackish. Twenty thousand people, give or take. The whole place floods a little, twice a day, and everyone has simply arranged their lives around that.

**The Hollow Gate** — two spans of black stone with no door, older than the city, at the landward road. Everyone touches the left pillar going under. Nobody knows who raised it. In chapter one I spell it *Hollowgate* once by accident — fix that.

**Ilva's Landing** — quay district, former shrine, former fish market, currently neither. Poor, loud, the part of the city that still works.

**The Ninth House** — four storeys of grey brick, cellar floods every tide. The Communion took it after the weir broke. Not the ninth of anything.

**The Gullet** — the tidal channel the city sits on. Fast, cold, brown.

**The Tally Office** — long, low, too many doors, where the Gullet narrows.

**The Weir** — upstream. Broke eleven years ago. (Check this against the draft — I think I wrote nine somewhere.)

---

## Groups

**The Tally Office** — a commercial and bureaucratic body; keeps the Long Tally, employs the wardens. Not a guild exactly, but it acts like one.

**The Marsh Watch** — the city's armed force. Answers to the Prefect. Martial, understaffed, resented.

**The Communion of the Drowned** — the church. Faith and belief. Holds the drowning-debt doctrine and most of the city's money.

**House Vashet** — a kinship group; three generations of Prefects. The office is not hereditary and has been hereditary for sixty years.

---

## Things

**The Long Tally** — eleven feet of hinged birchwood panels. Every debt in Saltmarch cut into it as notches and saltglass beads. You cut it; you do not write in it; the cut stays. It is simultaneously a ledger, an instrument and the antagonist.

**The saltglass knife** — Corvin's. Also the tool the Tally is cut with, which is the point.

**Halbrid's lantern** — hung from the bow of the ferry. Goes out in chapter two. It's a lantern.

---

## Ideas and vocabulary

**Tallywork** — the practice of keeping the Long Tally. A trade, a discipline and a worldview.

**The drowning-debt** — an obligation inherited from anyone who died in the water. The Communion's doctrine; the city's economy; the book's engine.

**Saltglass** — marsh glass, cloudy green, made where lightning strikes the flats. Used for beads, knives and windows.

**Gutterwitch** — local word for an unlicensed healer. Derogatory, then not.

**Sump-dark** — the particular quality of dark at low tide before dawn.

**Tithe-warden** — what the Communion calls its own collectors, to annoy the Tally Office.

---

## Events

**The Breaking of the Weir** — eleven years before the book opens. Flooded the lower city, killed several hundred, and — this is the actual plot — was followed by six months in which every account in Saltmarch was re-cut. Nobody asked who was holding the knife.

**Mirel's oath** — her swearing-in as warden, aged nineteen. Flashback material.
